              Quick guide

              Tap a heading to open it, or its Details to read it in a window (with a button to go to that tab). Most numbers update by themselves as you change things.

              Tapping to see more: anything with ▶ in front opens when you tap it (▼ once open; tap again to close): class sections, feats and traits in their lists, rules text, Browse all lists. Details buttons open a window with the full text and what adds to a number. Names of race traits and class features (in colour) open their text when tapped.

              Install it as an app (works without internet)
              • iPad / iPhone (Safari): open the site, tap the Share button, then Add to Home Screen.
              • Android tablet (Chrome): open the menu (⋮) and choose Install app or Add to Home screen.
              • Laptop (Edge or Chrome): click the install icon at the right end of the address bar, or open the menu and choose Apps → Install this site as an app.
              • It gets its own icon and opens full screen. After the first visit it works with no internet; when you're online it picks up updates by itself (close and reopen it to see them).
              • On an iPad or iPhone the home-screen app keeps its own saved characters, separate from Safari's. To bring a character over, Export it in Safari, then Import that file in the app.
              Saving, switching and printing characters
              • Everything saves automatically in this browser. Each device (laptop, tablet) keeps its own characters.
              • Use the Character list at the top to switch between characters; New, Duplicate, Delete and the other character buttons are on the title's line at the very top. With only one character saved, its name is shown there as plain text; the list appears once you have two or more. Type a Name to label one.
              • To move a character to another device: Export saves a file; on the other device, Import that file.
              • Print makes a one-page character sheet.
              • After an update, reload the page to get the newest version.
              Dice rolls
              • Any 🎲 Roll button rolls for you: saves, skills, ability checks, initiative, attacks, damage, spells, healing.
              • The result appears in a box at the bottom of the screen; the last few rolls are kept there.
              • Attack rolls spot natural 20s and 1s and critical threats, roll the confirmation and the extra damage.
              Search (the 🔍 Search button beside Print)
              • 🔍 Search, in the row with Print, opens a search window from any tab. It finds anything by name: races, classes, archetypes, feats, traits, skills, spells, gear. Tap a result to see it; most have a button to add it (e.g. Take an archetype or trait). Close the window with Close or by tapping outside it; your last search is still there when you open it again.
              Character tab
              • Popup windows (Details, the feat and trait lists) close with their Close button or by tapping anywhere outside them.
              • House rules: switch each one Y or N. Encumbrance (what you carry slows you), Max Starting Gold (a 1st-level character's starting gold is the most its class's roll can give, e.g. a barbarian's 180 gp instead of 105), Max Healing (healing rolls give their maximum), Action Points (Pathfinder hero points), Flaws (up to two, chosen from a list, each gives a bonus feat and its penalty is applied), Extra Campaign Trait (a third trait), Drawbacks (Paizo: take one drawback for an extra trait).
              • Race: tap any item (Medium, Darkvision...) to see what it does. Open Alternate racial traits to swap some traits for others; ones that clash are greyed out. Details on one shows it beside the standard trait it replaces, with Take or Remove.
              • Hit points: use − / + to set an amount (negative for damage), then Apply; Full heals completely. Xtra-HP lists the ways to go above your maximum: switch on Spirit Boost (an oracle's healing past your maximum becomes temporary hit points), or add temporary hit points from aid, false life, greater heroism, rage and more (roll or type the amount). Temporary hit points show beside your hit points and are lost first.
              • AC, CMD, CMB, saves and combat maneuvers are in the Race card, each with a Roll button.
              • On the Race card, Details beside each saving throw lists what adds to it: each class's base save, the ability modifier, feats, traits and active effects. The Details under AC shows every piece of AC and which ones count for touch and flat-footed AC; Touch, Flat-footed and CMD each have their own, and the one beside CMB explains CMB and CMD.
              • On the Results card, Details explains maximum hit points (hit dice, Con, favored class, Toughness), initiative, base attack bonus (each class's share), speed (with fast movement for barbarians, bloodragers and monks), what you wear and your load.
              • Active effects has six lists to switch things on from: Spell adjustments (spells cast on you), Conferred abilities (rage, bardic performances, defensive stance, studied target, favored enemy, challenge), Other adjustments (belts, headbands, rings of protection, amulets of natural armor, cloaks of resistance, bracers of armor, with the bonus they give), Circumstances (aid another, favorable or unfavorable conditions, higher ground), Combat conditions (flanking, charging, fighting defensively, total defense, cover, shooting into melee, squeezing) and Conditions (shaken, sickened, fatigued, entangled, flat-footed, stunned, energy drained and more). Conditions that cost you your Dex bonus to AC, or halve your speed, are counted too. The lists hold every Core Rulebook condition and combat modifier, the polymorph spells (beast shape, elemental body, form of the dragon... pick the form in its list; your size becomes the form's), other spells with plain bonuses, class abilities that switch bonuses on (smite evil uses your Charisma, judgments, mutagens, animal focus, banners) and magic items with fixed bonuses, including items that help one skill (cloak of elvenkind's +5 Stealth shows on the Skills tab). Each entry's Details shows the spell, item or class text it comes from.
              • Active effects: switch on spells cast on you (bless, haste, bull's strength, mage armor, barkskin, enlarge person...) or class abilities (rage for barbarians and bloodragers, the unchained barbarian's rage, a skald's inspired rage, inspire courage) and set the caster level where it matters. Add your own for anything else (name, bonus, type, what it affects, including Melee attack rolls or Melee damage rolls only; + another bonus gives one effect several, like a rage's Str, Con, Will and AC ; All d20 rolls covers attacks, saves, skills and ability checks). Every number and roll in the app (scores, AC, attacks, damage, saves, skills, ability checks, initiative, CMB, speed) counts them, following the stacking rules: bonuses of the same type don't add up, only the highest counts (dodge and untyped ones do add up). Details on a spell shows what it gives at your caster level, whether each bonus counts next to your other effects, how it grows, and the spell's own text. A custom effect's Details shows everything it applies to and whether it counts.
              Abilities tab
              • Ability scores: point buy with − / +; pick where racial +2s go and your ability increases at 4th, 8th... level. Each score shows its base, racial change, level increases, total and modifier; ability checks can be rolled here. Details on a row shows how the score adds up (point cost, race, increases, and each active effect with whether it counts), the ability check with a Roll button, and what the ability is used for, including its skills.
              • Point buy: Low (10), Standard (15), High (20) or Epic (25) points, scores 7 to 18. Custom is for rolled scores or ones your GM gives you: type each base score in its box (3 to 25, or use − / +); no points are counted. Switching back to a point buy brings any score outside 7–18 into range.
              Classes tab
              • Classes: one line per class, showing how many levels you have in it. Use Add a level of to add one (a class you already have joins its line; multiclassing works). Tap a class line to see each of its levels with the choices made there (favored class bonus, feats, traits, ability increases), add or remove a level of it, or change it to another class. In your favored class, choose +1 hit point, +1 skill rank or your race's option at each level.
              • Start over as a class, then Start over: every level is removed and the character becomes a 1st-level character of that class (its favored class too). Race, ability scores, feats, traits and gear stay. (The last level can't be removed on its own: a character always has at least one.)
              • Details on a class line, on a class's section, or beside Add a level of (for the class chosen there) shows the class: book, hit die, skill ranks, alignment, class skills, requirements for a prestige class, and its level table (base attack, saves and features) with your levels marked, with Add a level and Remove a level. Details beside Favored class shows what your favored class bonuses add up to and every favored class option your race has.
              • Open a class's section to add archetypes and to see its features by level: tap any feature to read what it does. Struck-out features were replaced by an archetype; coloured ones come from it. The Also row lists features the level table doesn't name (a cleric's spontaneous casting, proficiencies, bonus languages...).
              • Rangers pick a Combat style (archery, two-weapon combat and 25 more): its combat style feat slots (2nd, 6th, 10th, 14th, 18th level) then offer only that style's feats (more join at 6th and 10th), without their prerequisites.
              • Clerics, warpriests, paladins and oracles can pick a Variant channeling (optional, Ultimate Magic) after their deity's portfolio, or keep standard channel energy; its Details shows the heal and harm effects.
              • Under the archetype list, Browse all … archetypes lists every one with why it can't be taken (if it can't); Details on a row, or on an archetype you've taken, shows its features, the level each comes at and what it replaces, with Take or Remove.
              • Clerics choose two domains and inquisitors a domain or inquisition in their class's section. A druid's Nature bond there is an animal companion or a domain; a domain adds a domain spell each spell level. Browse all lists every domain the class can choose (with why one can't be chosen); Details on a row or on a chosen domain shows its powers, the level each starts at and its domain spells, with Choose or Remove.
              • Each class's section opens by itself while it has choices to make (its line says how many): tap the class's line to open or close it.
              • Classes that make a one-time choice pick it in their section: a sorcerer's or bloodrager's Bloodline, a wizard's Arcane school (focused and elemental schools too, then two Opposition schools for a specialist), a witch's Patron, a shaman's Spirit, a cavalier's or samurai's Order, and a summoner's Eidolon base form (the unchained summoner also picks the Eidolon subtype). Details (and Browse all, with Details on each) shows its book, text, class skills, bonus spells, hexes, edicts and other facts, and every power with its level, with Choose. Once chosen, its powers are listed by level, the class table names them (bloodline powers and spells, spirit abilities, order abilities, the school and order), its class skills count as class skills, the Spells tab lists bloodline, patron and spirit magic spells, bloodline feat slots take only the bloodline's bonus feats, and the printed sheet lists the choice. With the Crossblooded archetype (sorcerer) or Crossblooded Rager (bloodrager), pick a Second bloodline too, then a bloodline power at each power level (one of the two new ones, or an earlier one not yet taken). Both bloodlines' class skills and bonus feats count; −2 on Will saves is applied, and a crossblooded sorcerer knows one fewer spell of each level.
              • Class choices: classes that pick something at set levels have a list in their section, one line per pick: rage powers (barbarian, skald), rogue talents (advanced ones from 10th level; shadowdancers too), ninja tricks, slayer and investigator talents, hexes (witch: major from 10th, grand from 18th; shaman: shaman and spirit hexes), alchemist discoveries, magus arcana, the oracle's curse and revelations (choose the Mystery first: its Details, and Browse all with Details on each, show the mystery's book, deities, class skills, bonus spells, revelations with their text, and final revelation, with Choose; the curse and the revelations have Browse all with Details on each too: Choose there fills your first empty pick it fits), arcanist exploits, vigilante and social talents, kineticist infusions and wild talents, paladin mercies, antipaladin cruelties, unchained monk ki powers and style strikes, phrenic amplifications, mesmerist tricks and bold stares, occultist focus powers, shifter aspects, the bard's and skald's versatile performance, ranger favored enemies and terrains, fighter weapon training groups, the unchained rogue's edge, and the prestige classes' defensive powers, inspiring commands, loremaster secrets and rage prophet spells. Choose opens a searchable list with each option's text; ones you can't take yet are greyed with the reason. Each has Details: its full text, book and level, with Choose and Back to the list. Details on a chosen one has Change and Remove. Feats such as Extra Rage Power, Extra Rogue Talent, Extra Hex, Extra Discovery, Extra Arcana, Extra Revelation, Extra Mercy and the other Extra … feats for these choices add one more pick each time they're taken, marked with the feat's name and the class level it counts as (the level when the feat was taken: an option that needs a higher level needs the feat in a later feat slot). An archetype that replaces picks marks them "replaced by" it; other changes it makes (like extra options) are listed under Your archetype changes these. Choices show in the class line's level popup and on the printed sheet. They aren't added to the numbers: use a custom effect for bonuses that apply.
              • Animal companion (below the Classes card) appears for druids, hunters, rangers (from 4th level) and clerics or druids with the Animal domain. Choose the animal and name it: hit points, AC, saves, attacks, skills, feats, tricks and its special abilities follow your level (class levels that give a companion add up), with Roll buttons and Details. It prints on the sheet. Browse all (or Details beside the animal) shows each animal's starting statistics, what changes when it advances, and what it would be at your level, with a Choose button. Each trick has Details too: what the animal does and the Handle Animal DC to teach it. Attack (all creatures) is Attack taught a second time (it counts as two tricks) so it attacks undead, aberrations and other unnatural creatures too.
              • Companion feats: Weapon Focus and Improved Natural Attack get a second list to pick the natural attack (+1 on that attack; its damage dice one step bigger). Barding puts armor on the animal: its armor bonus, Dex limit and check penalty count (on attacks too without the Armor Proficiency feat for it), medium or heavy barding slows it, and its price (more than a person's armor) comes out of your gold on the Equipment tab.
              • Feats that need an animal companion (Boon Companion...) count any class feature that gives one: a ranger's hunter's bond, a hunter, a druid's nature bond.
              Skills tab
              • Add ranks with − / +; the count at the top shows how many you have left. Class skills get +3 once they have a rank.
              • Roll works with no ranks too (just the ability modifier and bonuses). By the rules, trained-only skills need a rank, except Knowledge (DC 10 or lower), Handle Animal (domestic animals) and Sleight of Hand (DC 10 or lower); the others still have a Roll button, for groups that allow it.
              • Details beside the ranks count shows the ranks each level gives (class ranks plus Int, a human's Skilled, favored class bonus) and where you've spent them.
              • Details shows everything that adds to a skill: ranks, ability, class skill, race, feats, traits, armor, size (Stealth and Fly) and active effects, then what the skill does, as the Core Rulebook gives it: how to use it (with its DC tables), the action it takes, whether you can try again, special rules and untrained use.
              • Craft, Perform and Profession each have a + Add button: it opens a list to tap (the crafts from the Core Rulebook and later Paizo books, the nine kinds of performance, the Core Rulebook's professions), or type any other and press Add. Each one becomes its own skill row for ranks.
              Feats tab (feats, traits and flaws)
              • At the top of the Feats card, each feat you've chosen is on one line (✓ / ✗, the feat, its slot) with Details.
              • Details beside the Feats count lists every feat slot (the level it comes at, where it comes from, the feat in it with ✓ / ✗) and your free class feats; tap a free feat's name to read it. Details beside the Traits count shows how many traits you get and why, each trait's category (the rules allow one per category) and what's counted.
              • Each feat slot shows the level it comes from; Choose a feat opens a list you can search and filter. Green ✓ / red ✗ show whether you meet each prerequisite.
              • Feats like Weapon Focus or Skill Focus ask which weapon or skill; that choice is applied automatically.
              • Details on each feat slot says where the slot comes from, checks the prerequisites at the level the feat was taken, and tells you what the app counts for that feat (or that you apply it in play). In the feat list, Details on a row shows the feat in a popup with a Choose button.
              • Traits: choose two (three with the Extra Campaign Trait house rule). Their bonuses are added for you; Details shows each bonus and whether it counts (two trait bonuses to the same thing don't stack). In the trait list, Details on a row shows the trait, what it would count alongside your other trait, and a Choose button. Each trait must come from a different category (combat, faith, magic, social, race, regional...): traits from a category you already have are greyed out with the reason.
              • Flaws and Drawback cards appear when those house rules are on: choose from the list to see the full text. Flaw penalties count in your numbers and rolls; a drawback adds a trait slot (its penalty is applied in play). Details beside a choice, or in Browse all, shows the full text, what the app changes and the feat or trait it gives.
              Armor tab
              • The Armor and Shield cards start with one line (what you wear, its bonus, Dex limit, check penalty, spell failure); Details at the end opens the choices and full stats. With nothing worn, they stay open.
              • Details beside the armor or shield opens it (stats, quality and material, Update, add special abilities, take it off). Beside Quality: what masterwork and +1 to +5 do and cost for it, and its price so far. Beside Material: what the chosen material changes compared with the usual one, and every material it can be made of.
              • Choose armor and a shield, and their Quality (masterwork or +1 to +5). AC, Dex limit, check penalty, spell failure and speed update for you, with warnings if you aren't proficient.
              • Material makes it mithral, adamantine, darkwood, dragonhide and so on (only the ones that item can be made of are listed); price, weight and stats change to match. Dragonhide, darkwood and the leathers are fine for druids.
              • To add special abilities (fortification...), use Add magic gear on the Magic Items tab.
              • All armor and shields at the bottom (tap its heading to open it) lists every one by category. Tap one or its Details to see it, pick a quality (masterwork or +1 to +5) and material, and wear it; or go straight on to Add special abilities (bought) or Craft it magic.
              • Details buttons show how the price, AC, Dex bonus, check penalty, spell failure and speed are worked out.
              Weapons tab
              • Each of your weapons is one line: its name, Attack, Damage and Critical, each with a Roll button, and Details at the end, which opens everything else below it (what adds to each number, range, cost, size, quality, special abilities, feats, remove). The line's text gets smaller to stay on one line. ⚠ means you aren't proficient or the weapon is too big to wield.
              • On each weapon, Details beside its name opens the weapon; beside Size it shows what a weapon made for another size changes (dice, attack, hands); beside Quality, what masterwork and +1 to +5 add and cost; beside Strength rating (composite bows), how much Strength it adds; beside Range, the penalty at each range increment; beside Cost, how the price adds up.
              • Material makes a weapon of a special material: adamantine, cold iron, alchemical silver, mithral, darkwood and the rest of the Core Rulebook and Ultimate Equipment list (bone, bronze, gold, obsidian and stone too). Only the materials that weapon can be made of are offered. Price, weight, the +1 on attacks of materials that are always masterwork, and damage changes (silver −1, bone and gold −2) are counted; what it does in play (bypassing damage reduction, extra damage) is shown under it.
              • The weapon list includes later books' weapons (Adventurer's Armory 2, Inner Sea Combat, Weapon Master's Handbook...), such as the butchering axe.
              • Pick a weapon in the list below, then Add it (or press Details on its row to see it in a popup and add it from there). Each weapon shows its attacks, damage and critical with Roll buttons; Details beside the attack lists everything that adds to it, the one beside the damage shows the damage and special abilities, and the one beside Critical explains the threat range, multiplier and critical damage.
              • Size picks a weapon made for a Small, Medium or Large creature (normally your own size). A different size uses that size's damage dice, gives −2 on attacks per size step, and changes how many hands it needs (a Large longsword is two-handed for a Medium character); a Large weapon costs and weighs twice as much. The Titan Mauler (barbarian) and Titan Fighter archetypes' big-weapon abilities are applied (Massive Weapons, Jotungrip, Giant Weapon Wielder, Incredible Heft); a Titan Mauler gets a Jotungrip tick box on two-handed weapons.
              • Wherever you pick special abilities (weapon cards, Add magic gear, the Craft tab), Browse all lists every one and Details shows what it does, its price, aura, caster level and what making it needs, with an Add button.
              • Quality sets masterwork or +1 to +5; Special abilities (flaming, keen, holy...) add their damage automatically. Situational ones (holy against evil) get their own Roll button.
              • Combat options at the top: Power Attack, Deadly Aim, Rapid Shot (once you have the feats) and two-weapon fighting. Details shows what each switch does at your base attack bonus, and how each hand's attack and the two-weapon penalties are worked out.
              • A composite bow has a Strength rating (set when you add it to match your Strength bonus): it adds your Strength bonus to damage up to that rating, costs 100 gp (longbow) or 75 gp (shortbow) per point, and gives −2 on attacks if your Strength bonus is lower. An adaptive bow always matches your Strength. Other bows add only a Strength penalty.
              Spells tab
              • In My spells, each spell is one line (its main numbers with Roll buttons); Details opens the rest.
              • Prepared today (wizards, clerics, druids, magi and other casters who prepare spells): one slot for each spell you can cast today at each level (bonus spells, and a wizard's school or a cleric's domain slot, included). Pick a spell for each slot (wizards and other spellbook casters from My spells; clerics and druids from their whole list, My spells first; a lower-level spell can go in a higher slot), tick cast when you use it, and press New day after resting to clear the ticks. About opens the spell. The choices are saved with the character.
              • Under each class in Spells per day, a line gives your caster level, concentration, save DC and spell resistance check; its Details shows how each is worked out. In My spells, Details beside a spell opens it (with Remove), and beside a level's count it shows how many of that level you can know or prepare.
              • Shows spells per day for each casting class (Details on each row: the class table, bonus spells from your ability score, a domain or school slot, and the score needed to cast), and the class's spell list. Choose a spell and press Add to my spells (spontaneous casters: add the spells you know). Details on a row opens the spell in a popup, with its Add (or Remove) button.
              • My spells show attack bonus, damage, save DC and healing at your caster level, with Roll buttons and a spell resistance check. Details shows how the attack bonus and save DC are made up, and how the damage grows.
              Magic Items tab
              • In My magic items, each item is one line (slot, how many, cost, weight); Details opens how many, the cost breakdown and the item itself.
              • In My magic items, Details beside an item opens it (with One more and Remove); beside a potion, scroll or wand it shows how it's used, its caster level, save DC and price. Details beside the count shows which body slot each item fills, and warns when two compete for one slot.
              • Browse by category or search; choose an item to see it, then Add it (pick a version, e.g. +2, if it has one). Details on a row opens the item in a popup, with its Add and Craft buttons.
              • Add magic gear is for treasure and purchases at market price, no feats needed: make a weapon or armor magic, or add potions, scrolls and wands of any spell.
              • My magic items lists what you own, with − / + for quantities; Details shows how each cost is worked out (market price, or the cost to make for crafted items).
              Craft tab
              • Details beside What to make lists each kind of item with the feat it needs (✓ if you have it), the lowest caster level and how it's priced; beside Enhancement bonus, each bonus's price, cost and caster level; beside Caster level, what it changes; beside the weapon, armor or spell, that item; beside the requirements count, how unmet requirements raise the DC.
              • For making items yourself with the item creation rules: magic weapons and armor, potions, scrolls, wands, wondrous items, or any listed item (press Craft this item in its details on the Magic Items tab).
              • Wondrous item in What to make: type part of a name in Search (the list narrows as you type) or pick a Slot (belt, head, neck... or slotless), then choose the Item; Details shows its full text. Items that come in versions (a cloak of resistance +1 to +5) have a Version list.
              • It checks the feat you need and each requirement (✓ / ✗; tick "I meet this" for ones it can't judge), and shows the Spellcraft DC, your Spellcraft with a Roll button, the cost (half the price) and the time. Rush it halves the time for +5 DC. Details beside the price, cost, time and DC shows how each is worked out.
              Equipment tab
              • In Inventory, each item (and the armor and shield you wear) is one line: how many, cost, weight, with Details at the end to open how many (− / +), the cost breakdown and the item itself.
              • Details beside Gold shows your class's starting gold and the wealth-by-level table; beside Carrying capacity, the light, medium and heavy loads for your Strength and size, what each does, and your load now.
              • Set your gold, or use your class's starting gold or the wealth for your level.
              • Add gear from the list below (or press Details on its row to see it in a popup and add it from there). Totals show what you've spent (armor, weapons, magic items and gear) and what's left, plus the weight you carry (and your load, with the Encumbrance house rule). Details beside each total lists the items in it, and each inventory row has one for its price and weight.
